# Sprite specification — Last Call (ChatGPT / DALL·E 3)

Companion to `docs/STYLE_GUIDE.md`. Every asset needed, where it goes, and a ready-to-paste prompt formatted for DALL·E 3 via ChatGPT.

**Before generating anything, paste the "chat opener" message from `STYLE_GUIDE.md` once at the start of a fresh ChatGPT conversation.** Then send each prompt below in the *same chat*. Style locks via repetition + memory; there is no seed.

---

## DALL·E 3 sizes — what you actually get

DALL·E 3 only renders three aspect ratios:

| Size | Use for |
|------|---------|
| `1024x1024` | All portraits, all UI sprites, logo |
| `1792x1024` | Landscape backgrounds (switchboard, title, endings) |
| `1024x1792` | Not used in this game |

After download, the in-game display size is set in Godot. Portraits will be shown at ~256×256 inside the CallerCard; backgrounds at 1280×720. Don't downscale before importing — let Godot handle it (it'll look sharper).

**Transparency:** DALL·E 3 always renders solid backgrounds. That's *fine* for our portraits and backgrounds — they're meant to be solid teal-and-amber illustrations, displayed inside a framed CallerCard. For UI sprites (sockets, jack, patience light) where you'd normally want transparency: see "UI sprites" below.

Daniel is the most-used character; getting his silhouette right anchors every other portrait. Iterate as many times as needed here before moving on.

```
Aspect ratio: 1024x1024.

Olly Moss-style poster illustration. Flat fields of colour, two
dimensions, no photography, no realism.

Foreground: a pure black silhouette of a man in profile, baseball cap
with a curved brim, holding a 1985 telephone receiver pressed to his
ear, shoulders slightly hunched. He is rendered as a completely flat
black shape — no face, no skin, no eyes, no fabric folds, no internal
detail of any kind, just a clean cutout outline. The contour clearly
reads as: baseball-cap brim, head, neck, shoulder, jacket, raised arm
holding receiver.

Background: a deep teal night sky #0F2A33 fills 70 percent of the
frame. In the middle distance behind the man, a single small warm
amber light source (a distant gas station lamp post or window) glows
honey-coloured #E8B86A, casting a thin amber rim along the top-right
edges of his cap and shoulder. The bottom 20 percent of the frame is
a darker teal #173E4A horizon.

Composition: man's silhouette centered, taking up roughly 40 percent
of the frame, plenty of negative space around him. Looks like a single
plate from a 1985 silent-storybook. Painterly grain texture, like
gouache on paper.

No text in the image. No other people.
```

**Calibration checklist** before moving on:
- ✅ Is the figure a pure flat black shape with zero internal detail?
- ✅ Is the background deep teal-black, NOT bright cyan?
- ✅ Is the light source soft amber, NOT red/orange neon?
- ✅ Does it look painted, NOT photographed?
- ✅ Is there an amber rim only on the upper-right edges?

If any of those fail, regenerate with a direct correction message:

> "The figure has visible facial features — regenerate as a completely flat black silhouette with no internal detail at all, like a paper cutout."

> "Too photographic — regenerate as a flat illustrated poster in the style of Olly Moss, with hand-painted texture and no realism."

> "The colours are too saturated — regenerate with a deep teal-black background (#0F2A33) and a soft honey amber light source (#E8B86A), not neon."

Don't move on to the next character until Daniel is right. He's the anchor.

## UI sprites — DO NOT use DALL·E for these

Sockets, the cable jack, and the patience light are small (≤96px), need transparent edges, and need to look identical to each other. DALL·E 3 is the wrong tool. Three options, easiest first:

### Option A — keep the placeholders (recommended for jam scope)
The current `ColorRect` socket boxes and project-icon jack already read clearly. With the new background and portraits in place, the placeholder UI looks intentional ("retro game look") rather than unfinished. **Spend zero time here and ship.**

### Option B — quick hand-paint
Use any free pixel/raster tool (Aseprite trial, Pixelorama free, Krita, or even Figma) to draw:
- `socket_lit.png` 96×96: a brass ring with an amber-glowing hole
- `socket_dim.png` 96×96: the same brass ring, no glow
- `cable_jack.png` 64×64: a small red rubber plug with a brass tip
- `patience_light.png` 32×32: a mint-green dot with a brass collar

15–30 min each in any tool. The shapes are simple enough that hand-painting beats AI anyway.

### Option C — generate at 1024×1024 then crop/mask in Godot
If you really want DALL·E to do it, prompt for *one* sprite per generation against a solid teal background, then import to Godot and use a shader or a `TextureRect` with a circular mask to crop. More work than Option B for less clean results.

## Workflow (ChatGPT-specific)

1. **Fresh ChatGPT chat.** Paste the opener from `STYLE_GUIDE.md`.
2. **Generate `daniel.png` first.** This is your style calibration. Tweak in chat until it nails the look.
3. **Once `daniel.png` is locked, blast through Tier 1 in the same chat session** without leaving. Each new prompt references the previous as "same style as before". Don't open a new chat unless drift becomes uncorrectable.
4. **Right-click each image → "Save image as"** into `art/portraits/` or `art/backgrounds/` with the exact filename from the file layout above.
5. **Log every generation in `docs/AI_PROMPTS.md`** (filename, date, prompt summary, any iteration notes). This feeds `ATTRIBUTIONS.md` for the jam submission.
6. **Hook into the game.** In `tools/generate_calls.py`, you'll add `caller_portrait="art/portraits/daniel.png"` on Daniel's calls (and equivalents on the others). Then regenerate the `.tres` files. I'll wire the loading code when you have the first portrait ready.
7. **Don't try to finish all art in one go.** Tier 1 is enough to massively improve the look. Tier 2/3 are nice-to-have for jam scoring on Aesthetics.
